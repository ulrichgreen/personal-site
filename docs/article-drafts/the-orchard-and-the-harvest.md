# The Orchard and the Harvest

> Most ideas should not be built. The system that knows this keeps an orchard: a wide field of captured ideas, grazed occasionally, harvested rarely, and never mistaken for a plan.

**Description.** A small essay on idea husbandry. The failure mode of idea management isn't losing ideas — it's promoting them too early, so the backlog fills with half-commitments that guilt-trip every planning session. The alternative is a deliberate gradient: a raw inbox (capture everything, judge nothing), an orchard (ideas worth keeping alive, each with a one-line note on why it fits the direction), and a harvest (the roadmap — only what's actually next). Movement between tiers is the *whole* discipline: ideas graduate rarely, on merit, and graduated ideas leave the orchard so it never becomes a second roadmap.

**Why it lands.** Everyone's ideas file is either a graveyard or a source of ambient guilt. The orchard reframe removes the guilt structurally: an idea sitting unharvested for years is the orchard *working*, not failing — most of the value is in having a wide field to choose from, and in every choice being deliberate. The judging filter travels well too: each idea carries a note on why it fits the direction, and "an idea that cannot justify itself against the manifesto does not belong on the roadmap, however clever it is." Idea management as an editorial act, not a productivity system.

## Outline

1. **The guilty backlog.** Four hundred tickets, median age three years. Nobody can delete them (they might be good) and nobody will do them (they aren't next). The list has become a debt ledger for imaginary promises.
2. **Capture is not commitment.** The raw inbox exists so ideas don't interrupt work — capture in full sentences, judge later, never auto-drain. The mistake is letting captured mean *accepted*.
3. **The orchard.** Ideas worth remembering, grouped, each with one line on why it fits the direction. Meant to be grazed, not read end to end. Deliberately broad, slightly redundant, and calm about the fact that most of it will never be built — that's the value, not the waste.
4. **The filter is the direction.** Every orchard entry justifies itself against what the project is trying to be. Cleverness is not a qualification. The note forces the test at capture time, when rejecting is cheap.
5. **Graduation is rare and one-way.** When an idea earns building, it moves out — to the roadmap, or to its own design doc — and leaves the orchard. Tiers only mean something if things actually move between them; a tier nothing leaves is a label, not a stage.
6. **Prune on contact with reality.** When the direction shifts, orchard entries that no longer fit get cut without ceremony. The orchard serves the direction; it accumulates no seniority.
7. **Close.** Keep a wide field. Harvest almost none of it. The point of the orchard was never the fruit per tree — it's that when you do reach, you reach among living things you chose to keep.
