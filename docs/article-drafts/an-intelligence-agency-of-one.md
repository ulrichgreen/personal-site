# An Intelligence Agency of One

> The difference between information and intelligence is one annotation: *why this matters for what I'm actually deciding.*

**Description.** A tour of a self-built research pipeline that replaces newsletter-drowning: layered collection (curated feeds daily, targeted searches for what feeds structurally miss, deep dives on demand), cadence as distillation — the weekly pass hunts patterns across dailies, the monthly hunts slow shifts the weeklies missed — and the grounding move that makes it intelligence rather than a link pile: every surfaced finding is cross-referenced against your own live decisions and annotated with why it matters *to them*. Cheap models triage; frontier models synthesize; you steer.

**Why it lands.** Every senior engineer has the same guilty pile of unread newsletters, and the productivity genre answers with reading systems — better folders for the pile. This is a different claim: reading isn't the bottleneck, *relevance* is, and relevance is a function of your decision context, which no feed knows and you never wrote down. Readers get an architecture (tiers, cadences, grounding facts, delivery split between the phone ping and the durable file) that scales down honestly — even the manual weekend version of "annotate findings against your open questions" changes what reading is for.

## Outline

1. **The pile.** Forty newsletters, all excellent, all unread. The problem was never quantity of signal; it was that nothing connected the signal to anything I was deciding.
2. **Three layers of collection.** Curated feeds for the known-good sources. Targeted searches for what a fixed list structurally can't surface — the discussion threads, the competitor moves, the release nobody blogged. Deep dives, on demand, one domain at a time. Each layer catches a different shape of unknown.
3. **Cadence is distillation.** Daily: what's new. Weekly: patterns across the dailies. Monthly: the slow-moving shifts the weeklies missed. Each tier is a pass over the one below, hunting what finer granularity couldn't see — not a bigger digest, a different question.
4. **The grounding move.** Every finding is checked against the live decision docs — the open questions, the current bets — and annotated: *why this matters here*. A finding that connects to nothing gets dropped, however interesting. This annotation is the entire difference between a feed and an instrument.
5. **Grounding facts, written down.** The pipeline is only as good as what it knows about your world: stack, constraints, choices already made. Leave those implicit and it over-ranks news about vendors you don't use — the machine's judgment is capped by the context you bothered to record.
6. **Spend by altitude.** Collection is scripted; triage runs on a cheap model; synthesis gets the frontier model; direction stays human. Matching cost to cognitive altitude is the same discipline as any other architecture.
7. **Deliver twice.** The immediate ping for consumption, the dated file for the durable record. Reading and archiving are different needs; conflating them is how archives become unread inboxes again.
8. **Close.** You don't need to read more. You need a system that knows what you're trying to decide — and there's exactly one party who can build that.
