# Materialized, Escalated, Fizzled

> Everyone flags "trends worth watching." Almost no one checks, months later, whether their watching was worth anything. Keep the ledger.

**Description.** A small discipline with outsized returns: every signal you flag as "worth watching" goes into a ledger, and on a schedule each one gets graded — *materialized* (it happened; did you act in time?), *escalated* (it's growing; watch closer), or *fizzled* (it went quiet; say so and stop carrying it). The ledger converts trend-watching from a vibe into a calibrated instrument: you learn your own false-positive rate, which kinds of signals you over-weight, and — the uncomfortable one — which materialized signals you flagged correctly and still failed to act on.

**Why it lands.** Tech-radar content is an industry of predictions with no scorekeeping — the same voices confidently flag the next thing regardless of what happened to their last twelve next things. An individual keeping honest score is contrarian by mere existence. The essay's teeth are in the third category: *fizzled* is the grade nobody gives, because carrying dead signals costs nothing visible — except that your watchlist becomes noise, and noise is where real signals go to be missed. Readers can start the ledger in a text file, this week, with the three words as column headers.

## Outline

1. **The prediction with no scoreboard.** A year of "worth watching" flags. How many panned out? Nobody knows — the flags were write-only. Watching without grading is astrology with release notes.
2. **The three grades.** *Materialized:* it shipped, it stuck, it matters — and the follow-up question that stings: did the early flag actually buy you anything? *Escalated:* still moving; the flag renews with a sharper question attached. *Fizzled:* quiet for two cycles; retired, on the record, no shame.
3. **Grade on a schedule.** The monthly pass over past flags takes minutes and is the entire mechanism. Grading in the moment of news is easy; the discipline is returning to the flag *after* the news cycle forgot it.
4. **What the ledger teaches you about you.** Your over-weightings become visible (everything from one community materialized; everything about one topic fizzled). Calibration isn't a trait — it's a feedback loop you either build or don't have.
5. **Fizzled is the load-bearing grade.** Dead signals carried forward are watchlist debt: each one dilutes attention on the live ones. Explicitly retiring a signal is what keeps "worth watching" meaning something.
6. **Acting is a separate skill.** The ledger's cruelest lesson: signals you graded right and did nothing about. Early awareness without a decision hook (what would we *do* differently, and when?) is trivia with a timestamp.
7. **Close.** Keep the flags. Grade the flags. The point was never predicting the future — it's knowing how much to trust yourself when you try.
