# Open Questions Are Live Forks

> Most plans record what was decided. The more valuable index is the opposite one: every question still genuinely open, one line each, in one place.

**Description.** A practice essay: maintain a single index of live forks — the decisions not yet made — across every area of a system, with a hygiene rule that gives it teeth: only *genuinely* open questions may appear, and when one resolves, its answer dissolves into the direction text and the line disappears. The index isn't a parking lot or a someday list; it's an honest map of current uncertainty, updated in the same pass as the thinking that opens or closes a fork.

**Why it lands.** Teams and individuals both suffer the same two failure modes: phantom openness (relitigating what was settled, because nothing says it's settled) and phantom settledness (building on what was never decided, because nothing says it's open). One index kills both. The framing does real work too — calling them *forks* instead of questions makes the stakes legible: each line is a place where two futures still exist, and someone will eventually collapse it, deliberately or by accident. Pairs the discipline with its payoff: the index is what lets you defer well instead of anxiously.

## Outline

1. **The two phantom states.** A settled choice reopened for the fourth time; an unsettled one that three features quietly assumed. Both are failures of *knowing which questions are open*.
2. **The index.** Every live fork, one line, one place, each line pointing at where the tension actually lives. Reading it takes two minutes and tells you the precise shape of what you don't know — a rarer document than any roadmap.
3. **Genuinely open, or gone.** The hygiene rule that keeps it honest. Resolved items don't move to a "closed" section — they become direction text and vanish from the index. An index that accumulates is a graveyard; one that shrinks and grows is a pulse.
4. **Forks, not questions.** A question sounds like homework. A fork is two futures, still both possible, waiting for a collapse. Naming the stakes changes how seriously deferral gets treated — some forks are cheap to hold open; some are quietly making every dependent decision for you.
5. **Blocked-on is information.** The best fork lines name their dependency: *blocked on the session schema*, *awaits a second real market*, *waits on an owner*. A fork blocked on evidence is patience; a fork blocked on nobody is `every-unowned-question-answers-itself.md`.
6. **Same-pass maintenance.** The index is updated in the same edit that opens or closes a question anywhere else — not in a weekly grooming ritual. Rituals get skipped; passes don't.
7. **Close.** Confidence isn't having no open questions. It's knowing exactly which ones they are.
