# Audit Your Sources

> Your reading list was curated once and trusted forever. Sources rot: authors pivot, feeds break, and the blog you needed most was never subscribed at all.

**Description.** What happened when every source in a carefully curated research feed was audited against its actual output: hard signal ratios per feed, three tiers (keep, watch, cut) — and the findings that generalize. Author drift: writers you subscribed to for one topic quietly pivot to another, and the feed keeps delivering their new life to your old need. Structural faults: the wrong feed URL silently serving adjacent content for months. And the deepest one — the silence you can't see: zero coverage of the exact technology you'd just bet on, because no source covered it and absence doesn't ping. Curation isn't an act; it's a maintenance schedule.

**Why it lands.** Nobody audits their inputs — we audit code, dependencies, budgets, and then make decisions downstream of an information diet assembled in 2019 and never re-checked. Each failure mode lands as a small shock of recognition (everyone has a pivoted-author feed still delivering), and the counter-scan idea gives the essay its constructive edge: deliberately searching *outside* your trusted sources on named themes, because a curated feed can only show you what it's subscribed to — the coverage gap is structural, and the fix has to be too.

## Outline

1. **The audit nobody runs.** Dependencies get scanners, budgets get reviews — the reading list that feeds your technical judgment gets nostalgia. Run the numbers once: relevant items over total, per source, against what you're actually deciding.
2. **Author drift.** The design-systems writer now covers their agency business; the performance blogger writes about organizing; the architecture voice pivoted to a new obsession. None of them wronged you — people grow. Your subscription just didn't grow with them, and each stale feed is noise wearing a trusted name.
3. **Structural rot.** The feed URL that points at the wrong (broader) blog. The paywall that quietly went up. The newsletter masquerading as a feed. Mechanical faults, invisible unless you compare what you *think* a source delivers against what arrived.
4. **The silence you can't see.** The sharpest finding: zero items about the platform you'd just committed to — across the precise months the commitment was made. No source covered it; no alarm exists for absence. You can only notice a gap by asking "what *should* I have seen?" against your own decisions.
5. **Counter-scan.** The structural fix for structural blindness: named must-fire themes searched outside the curated list, on a schedule — release trackers, advisories, standards agendas. Not because your sources are bad; because subscription is a filter, and filters need a bypass you control.
6. **Ground the ranker.** If something (or someone) triages for you, it ranks by what it knows of your world. The audit found over-ranked vendor news for a stack not in use — fixed not by better ranking but by writing the actual stack into the grounding facts. Judgment is capped by context.
7. **Close.** Trust in a source is a dated assertion, like any other cache. Re-validate on a schedule, prune without sentiment, and keep one channel that answers to your questions instead of your subscriptions.
